## The story

A planner at a mid-sized installer near Munich. Tuesday, 8:40. Six new enquiries, two site visits this afternoon.

Enquiry from Frau Weber: "Family of four, detached house, want solar, maybe a battery. Bill attached." A short form, a photo of the bill, an address, perhaps a five-minute call.

## Inputs

| Input | Where from | Fixed or variable |
|---|---|---|
| Yearly use (e.g. 4,500 kWh) | Bill | Fixed |
| People in the house | Form or call | Fixed |
| Heating (gas, oil, heat pump) | Ask | Fixed today, open for the future |
| Electric car | Ask | Often "maybe next year", uncertain |
| Roof size, direction, shade | Satellite or site visit | Fixed, often not known yet [UNCONFIRMED: how often roof data is missing at first design] |
| Power price and feed-in rate | Bill, market | Fixed |
| Budget | Rarely said | Hidden, planner guesses [UNCONFIRMED: how planners handle budget] |
| Products in stock | Own catalogue | Fixed |

## Decisions

- Panel size: how much fits the roof, and how much will she use?
- Battery or none: is there enough evening use for it to pay off?
- Heat pump or none: is the boiler old? Is it affordable now?
- Charger or none: is the EV real?

Then products, price, a budget check and the offer.

## Why it hurts

All four decisions depend on each other. A senior planner does it from gut feel. A newer one takes much longer [UNCONFIRMED: real hours per design] and still second-guesses the battery. Other installers are quoting too, and the first credible offer often wins (Reonic brief p5). Then the homeowner asks "what if we skip the battery?" and half of it is redone.

## What the planner wants (Reonic brief p4)

- A solid first design in seconds that they can trust.
- Override anything; the final say stays with them.
- One sentence per choice that they can say to the homeowner.
- A warning when their own design is unusual ("bigger than 9 in 10 similar homes").
