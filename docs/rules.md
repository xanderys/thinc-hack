# Planner rules: the transparent recommender

Module `engine/app/planner_rules/`. Production runs **rules-4-readout** (`planner_rules.rules4`): the two installers' own sizing, read out from their signed designs as a few thresholds per installer, fitted on the fit half (654 new-system jobs, seed 42, the same ids as `engine/lab/split`). Each rule shows the homes it matches (n of N) and the share it holds out of fold (5-fold CV); rules under 30% out of fold are marked weak. PV is capped by the roof in whole 450 W modules, and the battery snaps to the installer's package on sale. Beside the design, **rules-2-physics** computes this home's economic optimum for the variants and the Value layer. The calibrated **rules-1** and the band table **rules-3** are comparison columns. Re-fit and print: `cd engine && uv run python -m app.planner_rules.rules4`; the section below is written by `rules4.write_docs()` from the same `rules_table()` that `recommend()` returns.

## The optimum beside the design: rules-2-physics (variants and the Value layer)
For each home: the house model's roof planes (LoD2 faces from GET /api/roof/model, `max_panels` per face, the shaded yield `yield_shaded_kwh_per_kwp` from the bDOM surface model where present, else `yield_kwh_per_kwp`), ranked by that yield (flat faces: modules on 15 degree south racks, an assumption); pvlib's hourly shape for each face's tilt and azimuth is scaled to the face's annual yield, so the sweep runs on the real per-plane hourly yield. Without the model, the roof lookup's segments; without an address, a typical-year default (south, 35 degrees, up to 30 kWp; all three are assumptions, the 30 kWp set at the zero-VAT limit for homes, UStG 12(3)). The heat pump is sized from the lead's heat demand, else its heating bill, else the envelope's design heat load (LoD2 faces x IWU U-values, DIN EN 12831 simplified), which is an upper bound (unrenovated U-values on the whole volume) and is carried as `heat_load_upper_bound_kw` when a bill exists. No heat pump while the year's heat is only a default. Then the hourly load (BDEW H25 scaled to use, EV from 18:00, heat pump from heat demand and seasonal COP 3.4). Then every PV size, filling the best planes first module by module (at most 40 steps), times every catalogue battery package plus none, times heat pump yes or no where the home burns gas or oil, runs through the hourly year with 20-year cash flows. The recommendation is the knee of the upper frontier of (capex, discounted 20-year value): the last design reached by steps that each return at least 1 euro per euro, the same as a marginal return above the 3% discount rate. The 20-year value is the money saved plus an independence premium (29 ct per kWh of household and EV use the home supplies itself, Kaps and Netessine, Operations Research); `npv_20y_eur` stays money only. A stated preference moves along the frontier: independence doubles the premium, payback and upfront drop it. Physical caps: the roof, DC/AC 1.1 to 1.3; HTW's storage rule is a warning. Run time about 0.5 s per home (1.5 s cold).

1. Fill the roof's best planes first, module by module, and stop at the last step whose 20-year value is at least its cost; never past the roof.
2. Take the largest battery package whose last step still returns at least its cost over 20 years, counting losses and a replacement at end of life; above HTW's 1.5 kWh per 1,000 kWh is flagged.
3. Offer a heat pump where the home burns gas or oil, the year's heat is known and the 20-year value with it beats keeping the boiler; size it at the lead's heat demand or heating bill over 2,000 full-load hours, else at the envelope's heat load, which is an upper bound.
4. An 11 kW wallbox when there is an electric car and no wallbox.
5. The inverter that puts the DC/AC ratio between 1.1 and 1.3, nearest 1.2.
6. The design is the knee of this home's cost-to-value frontier, where the 20-year value is the money saved plus an independence premium of 29 ct for each kWh not bought from the grid; a stated preference moves along it: independence doubles the premium, payback takes the shortest payback and upfront the most money per euro, both without the premium.

The exam is roof-blind by necessity: the data pack has no addresses, so every exam home runs on the typical-year default roof and no envelope. Live leads with an address are not: their planes, shade and envelope drive the design. For the layers view: `frontier(inputs, roof)` returns every swept row (`kwp, kwh, capex_eur, npv_20y_eur, autarky_share, payback_years, on_frontier, chosen, label`); `day(inputs, design)` returns the April-May weekday with the median PV hour by hour (`pv_kw, load_kw, battery_kw` positive discharging, `grid_kw` positive import, `soc_kwh`) and that night (`evening_kwh` from the last PV hour to the first next morning, `night_hours`, usable `battery_kwh`, `htw_cap_kwh`). `recommend()` adds `sim_inputs` (`pv_planes`, the plane fill) to pass into `simulate()` so the economics match the sweep.

## Interface
Production: `planner_rules.rules4.recommend(inputs, catalogue=None, overrides=None, roof=None, constants=None) -> {design, components, explanation, rules, version, rules_fired}`. `overrides`: a number fixes a size, null fixes none, "include" asks for it. `rules[].constants[]` carry `weak` and the out-of-fold support. The optimum beside it: `planner_rules.recommend` (rules-2-physics), `frontier()` and `day()` for the variants and the layers. Comparison columns: `planner_rules.calibrated` (rules-1) and `planner_rules.rules3`.
