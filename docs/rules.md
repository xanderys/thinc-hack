# Planner rules: the transparent recommender

Module `engine/app/planner_rules/`. `recommend()` is **rules-2-physics**: the design comes from this home's own hourly physics and sourced 2026 economics, with no fitted constants. The installers' designs are only the exam. **rules-1-calibrated** (four constants fitted on the experts) stays importable as `planner_rules.calibrated` for the comparison column.

## rules-2-physics
For each home: the house model's roof planes (LoD2 faces from GET /api/roof/model, `max_panels` per face, the shaded yield `yield_shaded_kwh_per_kwp` from the bDOM surface model where present, else `yield_kwh_per_kwp`), ranked by that yield; pvlib's hourly shape for each face's tilt and azimuth is scaled to the face's annual yield, so the sweep runs on the real per-plane hourly yield. Without the model, the roof lookup's segments; without an address, a typical-year default (south, 35 degrees, up to 30 kWp). The heat pump is sized from the lead's heat demand, else its heating bill, else the envelope's design heat load (LoD2 faces x IWU U-values, DIN EN 12831 simplified), which is an upper bound (unrenovated U-values on the whole volume) and is carried as `heat_load_upper_bound_kw` when a bill exists. No heat pump while the year's heat is only a default. Then the hourly load (BDEW H25 scaled to use, EV from 18:00, heat pump from heat demand and seasonal COP 3.4). Then every PV size, filling the best planes first module by module (at most 40 steps), times every catalogue battery package plus none, times heat pump yes or no where the home burns gas or oil, runs through the hourly year with 20-year cash flows. The recommendation is the knee of the upper frontier of (capex, discounted 20-year value): the last design reached by steps that each return at least 1 euro per euro, the same as a marginal return above the 3% discount rate. A stated preference moves along the frontier. Physical caps: the roof, DC/AC 1.1 to 1.3; HTW's storage rule is a warning. Run time about 0.5 s per home (1.5 s cold).

1. Fill the roof's best planes first, module by module, and stop at the last step whose 20-year value is at least its cost; never past the roof.
2. Take the largest battery package whose last step still returns at least its cost over 20 years, counting losses and a replacement at end of life; above HTW's 1.5 kWh per 1,000 kWh is flagged.
3. Offer a heat pump where the home burns gas or oil, the year's heat is known and the 20-year value with it beats keeping the boiler; size it at the lead's heat demand or heating bill over 2,000 full-load hours, else at the envelope's heat load, which is an upper bound.
4. An 11 kW wallbox when there is an electric car and no wallbox.
5. The inverter that puts the DC/AC ratio between 1.1 and 1.3, nearest 1.2.
6. The design is the knee of this home's cost-to-value frontier; a stated preference moves along it: independence to the largest system that still breaks even, payback to the shortest payback, upfront to the most value per euro.

The exam is roof-blind by necessity: the data pack has no addresses, so every exam home runs on the typical-year default roof and no envelope. Live leads with an address are not: their planes, shade and envelope drive the design. For the layers view: `frontier(inputs, roof)` returns every swept row (`kwp, kwh, capex_eur, npv_20y_eur, autarky_share, payback_years, on_frontier, chosen, label`); `day(inputs, design)` returns the April-May weekday with the median PV hour by hour (`pv_kw, load_kw, battery_kw` positive discharging, `grid_kw` positive import, `soc_kwh`) and that night (`evening_kwh` from the last PV hour to the first next morning, `night_hours`, usable `battery_kwh`, `htw_cap_kwh`). `recommend()` adds `sim_inputs` (`pv_planes`, the plane fill) to pass into `simulate()` so the economics match the sweep.

## Interface
`recommend(inputs, catalogue=None, overrides=None, roof=None, constants=None) -> {design, components, explanation, rules, version, objective, sim_inputs}`. `overrides`: a number fixes a size, null fixes none, "include" asks for it; the rest re-solves around it. `inputs["roof_model"]` (the /api/roof/model JSON) gives the planes; `inputs["envelope"]["design_heat_load_kw"]` (or `roof_model.envelope`) sizes the heat pump when no heat demand is given. `constants` is ignored by rules-2.
