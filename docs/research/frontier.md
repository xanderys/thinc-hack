# Frontier products and patterns (research, 7 Nov 2026)

## Where the market is
- Reonic AI chat (live since 22 Jun 2026): sizes PV, storage and heat pump from the installer's catalogue and past projects, flags missing data as a list, renders variants and a live PDF offer; agents on triggers (Sep 2026). No accuracy, time-saved, explanation or past-project comparison published.
- autarc (YC): LiDAR heat load, "Scotty" drafts quotes, "AI planning check" against stored rules marked coming soon; claims up to 12 h saved per heat pump project.
- perfectPV (€39): battery size from 12 months of smart-meter data; three homes at 5,500 kWh and 10 kWp need 6, 10 or 14 kWh depending on routine; says the 1 kWh per kWp rule misses at about €800 per unneeded kWh (pv magazine Jul 2026).
- Densys EasyPlan: PV plan under 2 minutes, offer in about 5 (pv magazine Oct 2026). Eturnity: automated strings and diagrams.
- 1KOMMA5°, Enpal, Thermondo: AI in energy management and trading, not quoting; 1KOMMA5° quotes arrive 3 to 5 working days after a 2-hour call.
- Aurora Solar (US): AI roof in under 10 s, matches or beats humans on 63% simple, 83% moderate, 76% complex roofs (vendor, 2023); Lead Capture AI turns an address into a design, 25% of openers become leads; $159 to $259 per user per month. OpenSolar "Ada" auto-designs free. Enerflo: lead to proposal in 90 s (vendor). UK heat pump quoters (Octopus, Aira, Heatable, Heat Geek): instant estimate, price locked after survey, none explains the sizing.
- No independent 2025 to 2026 benchmark of AI system sizing exists. That gap is a pitch point.

## Position against the frontier (what Reonic could ship that nobody has)
1. Sizing with proof: every number opens the most similar past jobs, the physics year and the economic optimum. Counters the measured 1.5x heat pump oversizing.
2. Honest measurement: held-out score next to the agreement between near-identical homes and two baselines.
3. One question chosen by value of information, in line with Reonic's own "when in doubt, ask the user" principle; Reonic today lists missing data.
4. Refinement by sentence with the numbers locked; Reonic, Aurora and Enerflo produce a PDF.
5. Copies the installer's best planner, not the company average (weight by outcome: signed, no change order).
What Reonic lacks that others have: homeowner self-serve design on the installer's site (Aurora), a price-lock promise (Aira), phone-LiDAR site capture as an input (autarc).

## Borrow list (UI and product patterns)
1. Recommendation note plus a "why" panel with numbered factor badges (Stripe Radar risk insights).
2. Top 3 to 5 comparable past jobs with "based on N projects" (Zillow comps).
3. Three confidence lanes: send, quick check, planner decides (McKinsey underwriting 2025; Roots 2025).
4. Every override saved as a training signal (do not claim until built).
5. Changes in conversation with the quote updating live (Salesforce Agentforce 2025; Reonic variants).
6. Sentence rewrites as pending suggestions to accept or discard, in place (Notion suggested edits; Apple Writing Tools, WWDC25).
7. Inline citations on every figure that expand to the source job or norm (Perplexity pattern).
8. Battery sized by routine, shown as the three-family contrast (perfectPV). Chrome dimmed so the note leads (Linear 2025 refresh); progressive disclosure (Apple HIG).

## Regulation and trust, one line for the stage
The installer signs the commissioning protocol (VDE-AR-N 4105 and 4100) and carries yield liability (OLG München 14 U 345/14); the AI is their drafting tool. EU AI Act: limited risk; Article 50 transparency applies since 2 Aug 2026, so AI-drafted homeowner text is labelled. KfW 458 needs DIN EN 12831 room-by-room sizing, which stays with the planner.
